# Skin in the Game — Nassim Nicholas Taleb  ·  prefix [SITG]
> Symmetry of risk: never let anyone reap upside without exposure to the downside, because hidden risk transfer corrupts both ethics and knowledge. Dominant lens when a decision involves agency, advice, forecasts, experts, or anyone urging action whose consequences they will not personally bear.

## Principles

### [SITG:01] Skin in the Game as filter  (Prologue)
- **Essence:** Having exposure to the real world and paying for consequences (good or bad) is what teaches; transferring risk also transfers away learning. Systems get smart by removing those prone to error, not by people learning from mistakes.
- **Decision move:** Demand the decider be exposed to the downside of the decision; treat "skilled explanation without exposure" as a red flag.
- **Applies / fails when:** Central to professional asymmetry (interventionists, bankers, regulators). Relax it for harmless opinions with contained consequences (fortune-teller, casual conversation).
- **Example:** Interventionistas advocate regime change from air-conditioned offices; failure costs them nothing, so they never learn (Libya → slave markets).

### [SITG:02] Symmetry — no Bob Rubin trade  (Prologue / Ch 1)
- **Essence:** The core asymmetry to kill is "heads I win, tails you (or the taxpayer) lose." Bob Rubin kept $120M while Citi was bailed out; he invoked "Black Swan." Hammurabi countered this 3,800 years ago: the builder dies if the house collapses.
- **Decision move:** Before any deal, locate who keeps the gains and who eats the losses. If they differ, the structure is corrupt and accumulates hidden tail risk until blowup.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies wherever a bonus period (yearly) is shorter than the blowup period (decade) — the agent will play the risk-transfer game.
- **Example:** Hedge-fund owner-operators with half their net worth in the fund "go down with the ship"; bonus-driven bankers do not.

### [SITG:03] Via negativa & "systems learn by removing"  (Prologue)
- **Essence:** We know wrong with more clarity than right; knowledge and robustness grow by subtraction. Evolution requires the risk of extinction — removal of the unfit, not addition of rules.
- **Decision move:** Prefer actions that remove (a bad option, a fragile component, an additive regulation) over those that add complexity with unseen feedback loops.
- **Applies / fails when:** Strong for robust system design and risk reduction; addition is justified only where it demonstrably reduces fragility.
- **Example:** Aviation got safe because bad pilots ended up at the bottom of the Atlantic (system filtering), not because individuals "learned."

### [SITG:04] The agency / advice problem  (Ch 1)
- **Essence:** "Giving advice" as a sales pitch is unethical; selling is not advice. Beware anyone who tells you an action is "good for you" when it is also good for them and the harm to you does not touch them.
- **Decision move:** Separate the seller from the advisor. Discount any recommendation from someone with no penalty for being wrong; weight a genuine user's endorsement heavily.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to one-shot transactions and unsolicited advice. Most everyday tradespeople (plumbers, dentists) do pay for their mistakes — agency problem is mild there.
- **Example:** "Rip them off, don't tick them off — every day a new customer is born," said the top white-shoe salesman unloading inventory on clients.

### [SITG:05] Ethical beats legal; equality in uncertainty  (Ch 1)
- **Essence:** The ethical is more robust than the legal — over time law should converge to ethics, never the reverse. Sharia's *gharar* and Antipater's stance: no party in a transaction should have certainty about the outcome while the other has uncertainty.
- **Decision move:** Aim for maximal transparency, even of intentions, beyond what the law requires; an informational edge that approaches certainty is theft.
- **Applies / fails when:** Full disclosure of *defects* is required; a weak edge (inside information where the future is still uncertain to both) is tolerable.
- **Example:** Rav Safra honored his silent initial price rather than take the buyer's raised offer — shame-free maximal transparency.

### [SITG:06] Scaling — ethics is local  (Ch 1)
- **Essence:** Things do not scale: a country is not a large city, the world is not a large village. Ethical rules are fractal but bounded; beyond a club-sized "we," people fight for self-interest. Kant's universalism is "great on paper, disastrous in practice."
- **Decision move:** Design governance bottom-up from the municipality; distrust universalist, abstract, scale-free prescriptions ("treat all people as one category").
- **Applies / fails when:** Below Ostrom's threshold community size people act as collectivists protecting the commons; above it the system rots. Federalism > centralized multiethnic states.
- **Example:** People get along as neighbors but not as roommates; the "Swiss" (faceless outsiders) sit outside the local ethical realm.

### [SITG:07] The minority rule  (Ch 2)
- **Essence:** An intransigent minority (~3–4%), evenly distributed and cheap to accommodate, imposes its preference on the flexible majority — because the intransigent will not budge and the flexible can. The aggregate average misleads you about who actually decides.
- **Decision move:** When forecasting adoption or social outcomes, find the most intolerant constraint and the cost asymmetry — not the majority preference. Decentralize to localize the minority's reach.
- **Applies / fails when:** Requires even spatial distribution and low extra cost of compliance; fails if the minority is ghettoized or compliance is expensive (10× cost).
- **Example:** Almost all U.S. drinks are kosher and 70% of NZ lamb to the UK is halal, despite tiny observant populations — kosher eaters won't eat non-kosher, others don't care.

### [SITG:08] Employees as a risk-management strategy  (Ch 3)
- **Essence:** Employees exist because they have skin in the game (a reputation and a payroll to lose) and are therefore dependable; contractors are free but can defect at the worst moment. "Someone employed for a while is giving you strong evidence of submission."
- **Decision move:** When you need dependability under emergencies/tradeoffs, you are buying owned behavior; when you need free judgment and risk-taking, recognize employees are by design unable to provide it.
- **Applies / fails when:** Coase's firm exists where transaction costs exceed control costs. The "companies person" (fear of all future employers) is even more controllable than the old company man.
- **Example:** The contractor pilot Bob defects for a richer Saudi gig the night before the flight; an employee pilot never would.

### [SITG:09] What you fear losing defines your fragility  (Ch 3)
- **Essence:** "What matters isn't what a person has, it is what he is afraid of losing." The more you have to lose, the more fragile and controllable you are — even a CIA director can be more vulnerable than a truck driver.
- **Decision move:** To gauge someone's true freedom/leverage, map what they fear losing (status, peer approval, job assessment), not their nominal power.
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal lever for understanding incentives and manipulation. The autocrat / "f*** you money" holder acts free precisely because he fears losing little.
- **Example:** Putin projecting "I don't care" reads as a free citizen confronting slaves who must calibrate every statement to ratings and committees.

### [SITG:10] The skin of others in your game  (Ch 4)
- **Essence:** Ethical and intellectual freedom requires the *absence of the skin of others* in your game. Family, friends, and reputation are hostages that force compromise; this is why society wants its moral heroes celibate.
- **Decision move:** To make uncompromised ethical choices, reduce hostages to fortune; when assessing why someone caves, look for whom they can be hurt through.
- **Applies / fails when:** Neither celibacy nor financial independence makes one unconditionally immune — pressure migrates to whoever is most vulnerable near you.
- **Example:** GM harassed Ralph Nader's mother at 3 a.m.; corporations prefer employees with families and large mortgages because they are easier to own.

### [SITG:11] Scars signal realness  (Ch 5)
- **Essence:** Risk-taking with visible cost (the magician's bleeding hand) makes a person "real." The public can detect front-office (risk-bearing) vs. back-office (non-risk) operators. Life is sacrifice and irreversible peril — the "experience machine" is not life.
- **Decision move:** Prefer the candidate/operator who has visibly bled and survived over the flawless one; treat blemishes and admitted losses as credibility, not disqualification.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to judging conviction and competence under uncertainty. "Scars signal skin in the game."
- **Example:** Trump's visible deficiencies and advertised bankruptcy made him *more* convincing to risk-taking voters than a polished operator.

### [SITG:12] The Intellectual Yet Idiot (IYI)  (Ch 6)
- **Essence:** A credentialed, label-driven class that prescribes to others, gets first-order logic right but fails on second-order effects, has no skin in the game, and has been historically wrong (low-fat diets, GMOs, interventions, portfolio theory). They mistake the ensemble for the sum of individuals.
- **Decision move:** Down-weight advice from those whose only proof is passing exams written by their peers; up-weight time-filtered "grandmother" wisdom and practitioners.
- **Applies / fails when:** A small minority but disproportionately influential in media, think tanks, social science. Marker: doesn't understand ergodicity; "doesn't even deadlift."
- **Example:** The IYI calls plebeian votes he dislikes "populism" and votes he likes "democracy"; believes in "one Ivy League degree, one vote."

### [SITG:13] Dynamic (ergodic) inequality  (Ch 7)
- **Essence:** People tolerate inequality from heroes/entrepreneurs (whom you can be a fan of) but resent the rent-seeker with *no skin in the game* who cannot fall. True equality is equality in *probability* — making the rich subject to the risk of exit, not merely lifting the bottom.
- **Decision move:** When designing fairness, target the absence of downside at the top (no-absorbing-barrier condition) rather than a static snapshot; force rotation, not redistribution alone.
- **Applies / fails when:** In dynamic economies (US) the rich rotate and wealth is positive-sum; in rent-seeking economies (entrenched France/Florence) wealth is zero-sum and resentment is justified.
- **Example:** Piketty measures static inequality and ignores whether the top are the *same people*; the gold-chained restaurant diner risks the soup kitchen, the tenured economist does not.

### [SITG:14] The Lindy effect  (Ch 8)
- **Essence:** For non-perishables (ideas, books, technologies, institutions), life expectancy *lengthens* with age conditional on survival. Time, operating through skin in the game, is the only judge of experts — solving the "who judges the judges" regress.
- **Decision move:** To estimate the durability/validity of a non-perishable, weight how long it has already survived; prefer the old solution over the novel one absent strong reason.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to ideas, customs, tools, readership; an idea survives if it is a good *risk manager* (not necessarily "true"). Excessive change-for-change's-sake defeats Lindy.
- **Example:** "Use laws that are old but food that is fresh." Grandmother's advice works ~90% of the time; psychology papers replicate <40%.

### [SITG:15] Green-lumber fallacy / don't look the part  (Ch 9)
- **Essence:** What you need to know to succeed in reality often differs from what looks essential to the intellect. Conditional on success, the person who does *not* look the part had to overcome perception — strong evidence of real skill filtered by reality.
- **Decision move:** Prefer the successful operator whose details you understand the *least* and who doesn't look the part; distrust the clear, jargon-fluent narrative that "convinces suckers."
- **Applies / fails when:** Holds where results come from direct contact with reality; reverses where there is hierarchy and standardized job evaluation (executives are supposed to look like actors).
- **Example:** Pick the butcher-looking surgeon over the silver-haired one; the green-lumber trader made a fortune not knowing "green" meant freshly cut.

### [SITG:16] Constructed vs. natural preferences  (Ch 10)
- **Essence:** Wealth strips the skin-in-the-game experiential mechanism; the rich substitute sellers' constructed preferences for their own, complicating life into misery. "If wealth is giving you fewer options, you're doing it wrong."
- **Decision move:** Strip out constructed preferences; ask whether a choice is yours or a salesperson's, and whether sophistication has crossed into negative utility (the inverted-U).
- **Applies / fails when:** "Only the rich are poisoned" — the poor are spared scamming by complication. Beware experts/consultants whose industry exists to swindle.
- **Example:** The Michelin tasting menu leaves you starving and faking interest; a $200 fresh pizza would beat it — you'd pay to *avoid* the constructed experience.

### [SITG:17] Facta non verba; own the enemy, no verbal threats  (Ch 11)
- **Essence:** Deeds precede and supersede words; a living, owned enemy (who knows the rules and that he owes you his life) is worth more than a dead one. Verbal threats reveal only weakness — never issue them.
- **Decision move:** Replace cheap talk and explicit threats with credible, demonstrated capability; control behavior through earned uncertainty about what you *could* do, not declarations.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to strategic deterrence and reputation. The camera (silent documentation) restores civil behavior without verbal threat.
- **Example:** The Assassins planted a dagger by the sultan's bed — no spoken threat — and lived peacefully with him thereafter.

### [SITG:18] Virtue requires costly, unpopular risk  (Ch 13)
- **Essence:** Virtue is what you do for the collective when it conflicts with your interest; it cannot be advertised, bought (simony/"charity" PR), or signaled safely. "Courage is the only virtue you cannot fake." If private actions contradict your stated ideas, they cancel the ideas, not the life.
- **Decision move:** Test claimed virtue by its cost to the claimant — does it risk reputation, share the downside, and hold up when unpopular? If it generalizes only as "everyone should," distrust it.
- **Applies / fails when:** Highest virtue is unpopular and private (Matthew 6); virtue-signaling mob behavior is vice (bullying + cowardice). Advice to the would-be world-saver: take bounded risk, start a business.
- **Example:** Susan Sontag denouncing "the market system" while living in a $28M mansion — claiming virtue without living its consequences.

### [SITG:19] Rationality = survival, judged in actions  (Ch 18)
- **Essence:** "Survival comes first, truth and understanding later." There is no rationality of *beliefs*, only of *actions* judged by whether they aid survival. Superstitions, distortions, and rituals that protect against tail risk are not irrational; standing in their way can be.
- **Decision move:** Judge people and policies by revealed preferences (what they pay/risk), not stated beliefs; do not strip "inefficient" heuristics that may be encoding survival.
- **Applies / fails when:** "Not everything that survives survives for a reason — but everything that survives survives for a reason." Religion exists to enforce tail-risk management across generations.
- **Example:** The Pope rushes to the best hospital, not a chapel — "atheist in action, religious in words"; constructive paranoia keeps Papua New Guineans from sleeping under dead trees.

### [SITG:20] Ergodicity and ruin  (Ch 19)
- **Essence:** Ensemble probability (100 gamblers once) ≠ time probability (you 100 times). If your path can hit an absorbing barrier (ruin), past/average odds do not apply and cost-benefit analysis is void. Every small repeated risk compounds toward ruin with probability approaching 1.
- **Decision move:** Before any cost-benefit, check for ruin/irreversibility along *your* path. In strategies that entail ruin, benefits never offset the risk — reject regardless of expected value. Increase risk after wins, cut after losses ("play with house money").
- **Applies / fails when:** The decisive lens whenever exposure repeats or is irreversible. Never compare a multiplicative/systemic/fat-tailed risk (pandemic, terrorism) to an idiosyncratic thin-tailed one (bathtub deaths). Worst case is never just *your* death — collective/systemic ruin dominates; courage = sacrificing your layer for a higher one.
- **Example:** Russian roulette has 83% gains and an "expected" $833k per shot — yet keep playing and you reach the cemetery with certainty; "never cross a river that is *on average* four feet deep."
